## What worked / what did not

**Worked**

- **Refusing a stop chosen for size, twice, on two contracts, under pressure to trade.** BNBUSDT at 12:00
  (764.61 structure vs 772.78 convenience) and ETHUSDT at 20:00 (2693.00 structure vs 2707.00 convenience,
  which would have turned a failing 1.34 into a passing 2.31). This is the single failure mode the stop
  rule exists to prevent, identified and named in the decision file both times.
- **Refusing to invent a target.** At 20:00, three of five contracts had no daily high above spot in 60
  days. Clearing the floor on SOL or BNB required a target with no structural basis, and the decision named
  that as the same defect as a stop chosen for size.
- **Treating a passing reward/risk as necessary, not sufficient.** XRP at 21:00 cleared 1.58 and was still
  declined on evidence independence. The floor was used as a filter, not as a trade-finder.
- **Recognising correlated evidence as one observation.** Five contracts that moved 5.1–8.3% on one 08:00Z
  impulse do not supply two independent categories via `trend` and `market_context`.
- **Volume read honestly against the desired conclusion.** XRP's breakout hour showed *contracting*
  participation, and that was reported as arguing against the long rather than omitted.
- **Carried-forward items tested and falsified.** ETH was re-derived at the new price (1.34 → 0.55) instead
  of re-argued; the in-progress BTC artifact was re-checked after close and confirmed.
- **P-001 and P-002 visibly in effect the same day they were approved** — explicit `timing_check` blocks in
  both evening decisions, `slot_coverage` in `stats.json`.

**Did not work**

- Scheduled execution: one slot missed outright, one slot's decision submitted 7 hours late (before the fix
  landed), review timing inconsistent across the day.
- The 12:00 decision file's thesis went into the permanent record containing a wrong causal claim. The
  correction is attached, but nothing in the layout points from a decision file to a later correction, so
  anyone reading `decisions/` alone still gets the wrong story.
- The `derivatives` category is structurally degraded on this host and there is no rule saying so; today it
  was handled by judgement alone.

## Hypotheses

Day 1. Zero closed trades, three decisions. Nothing here is a finding, and none meets the lesson bar
(3 trades or 5 decisions).

- *(n=3 decisions)* **The binding constraint may be target availability, not direction.** All three
  no-trades failed on where a stop or target may sit, not on which way to go; at 20:00 five categories
  agreed on up and there was still no trade. If most no-trades over the next week carry a reward/risk or
  no-structural-target reason rather than a conflicting-evidence reason, the practical question becomes
  whether this watchlist can produce a qualifying long at all on a breakout day.
- *(n=1 day, 5 contracts)* **The five-contract watchlist may behave as one instrument.** All five moved
  5.1–8.3% on one impulse, and three of five simultaneously had no overhead daily reference. If this holds,
  the effective sample size of a trading day is closer to 1 than to 5, and diversification across the
  watchlist is illusory. Countable over the next week: the spread between the best and worst contract's
  daily move.
- *(n=1 day, 4 elapsed slots)* **Scheduled runs are unreliable on this host.** One clean miss, one
  7.5-hour stall. Now directly measurable via `slot_coverage`; a week of readings will settle it.
- *(n=3 prints, 1 day, 2 contracts)* **The demo host produces isolated extreme wick prints, concentrated on
  BTCUSDT.** Two of three today were BTC, inside four hours. If BTC keeps producing them while the other
  four do not, "BTC is structurally unreadable on this host" moves from a per-check judgement toward a
  standing assumption — and the risk is not the levels, which are being excluded correctly, but a resting
  exchange-side stop, which cannot be.
