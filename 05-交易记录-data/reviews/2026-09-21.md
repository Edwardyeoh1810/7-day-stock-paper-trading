## Decisions reviewed

**`2026-09-21_check_1200` — `no_trade` on the five-contract watchlist. Sound decision, correctly reasoned.**

- *Was the evidence real and independent?* Yes. Six categories, five of them read directly from the demo
  host's own public endpoints (klines 1d/4h/1h, premiumIndex, openInterest, ticker/24hr) at 04:10Z, with
  figures specific enough to be checkable. Trend and price_action genuinely conflicted rather than being
  restatements of each other: daily structure was up on all five (4h EMA20 > EMA50 everywhere), while the
  completed 00:00–04:00Z 4h bar printed a 43–71% upper wick into the 20–30 day range high on all five, on
  1.09–1.48× average volume. Derivatives were at baseline and genuinely neutral; market_context was mildly
  bullish and so contradicted the short.
- *Was the entry condition applied correctly?* Yes. No two independent categories agreed on one direction,
  which is the rule's definition of a no-trade. The short was rejected for trading against a clear daily
  uptrend on price_action alone — the right call, and it applied the same bar to the short as to the long.
- *Was the stop placed on price structure?* It was, in the rejected candidate. The BNBUSDT long was the
  best candidate (strongest 24h move, tightest spread at 0.52 bps) and was refused because an honest stop
  below the last real higher low at 764.61 gave 1.45% risk, which put a 1.5R target near 794 — above the
  785 high that had just rejected the whole complex on expanding volume. The decision explicitly refused to
  tighten the stop to 772.78 to buy a smaller risk, naming it as choosing a stop to fit a position size.
  That is the single most important thing that went right today.
- *What happened next?* The conflict resolved upward. By 11:37Z all five had broken the level that rejected
  them (BTC through 81,888/81,981; ETH through 2,700 to a 2,850 high; BNB through 785 to 791.7; SOL to
  116.55; XRP to 1.4969), a 5–8% day across the complex. The long was there in hindsight. It was not
  takeable under the rules at 12:10 MYT, and this does not count against the decision. One favourable
  outcome foregone is not evidence about the rule; judged on what was knowable at 04:10Z the decision was
  correct, and it would have been correct even if the complex had broken down instead.

**Three setup checks (08:32, 08:51, 09:12 MYT) — infrastructure validation, not decisions.** These wrote

## Process errors

These matter more than the P&L, and day 1 produced three, all pointing at the same layer.

**1. The 12:00 run stalled for 7.5 hours between observation and decision (high severity, no position
impact).** The observation ran at 04:10:09Z (12:10 MYT, inside the 30-minute grace). The decision was only
submitted to the engine at 11:37:20Z (19:37 MYT) — about 7 hours after the slot's grace expired. Host clock
and demo exchange clock were independently compared and agree, so the timing is not in doubt. No position
was open, the action was `no_trade`, no order was sent and nothing was backfilled, so there is no
accounting consequence today.

The consequence if this recurs on an entry is concrete and serious. `paper-config.json` bounds readiness
age (24h) and quote age (10s), and `paper_ledger.py` enforces both — but nothing bounds the age of the
*evidence* behind a decision. A stalled entry would therefore have passed every engine gate: fresh
readiness, fresh quote, and a fill at the 84,655 market price, against a thesis, stop and target computed
at 81,427. That is a 3.96% error against a planned risk of 1.45% on the BNB candidate — the trade would
have been filled roughly 2.7 planned-risk units away from where its stop was reasoned, with the stop
already far out of the money and position size computed from the wrong distance. The gate exists for the
quote and not for the reasoning. → Proposal 1.

**2. The 16:00 slot was missed entirely.** No decision file, journal entry or evidence exists for
`check_1600`; it falls inside the 12:10–19:37 stall window. Day 1 slot coverage is therefore 1 recorded
decision out of 4 slots that were due and reviewable (00:00, 04:00 and 08:00 preceded or coincided with
repository setup that morning and are excused; 16:00 is not). → Proposal 2.

**3. This review itself ran off-schedule**, starting 19:41 MYT against a nominal 22:00. Combined with items
1 and 2, three of today's scheduled executions ran late, early or not at all. `automation-status.json`
records `scheduler_execution_verified: false` and notes that tasks only run while the Claude desktop app is
open, which is a plausible common cause, though today's evidence does not establish one mechanism (a stall
and a miss are consistent with the app being closed; an *early* start is not). Whatever the cause, slot
timing is currently unreliable and unmeasured, and an experiment whose premise is a fixed four-hourly
cadence cannot report its result honestly without knowing what fraction of slots actually ran. → Proposal 2.

**4. A factual error in the decision thesis, self-corrected the same day (low severity, credit due).** The
thesis asserted a material venue divergence — demo host BTC 81,427 against third-party reporting near
85,000 — and treated it as a demo-environment data-quality caveat. That was wrong: the gap was time, not
venue. The demo host itself quoted 84,696 at 11:37Z, matching the reporting. The run caught this without
prompting and wrote the correction into
`evidence/2026-09-21_check_1200-research.json` and the companion research journal rather than editing the
generated decision file. That is the right handling — the immutable record stays immutable and the
correction is attached.

Judged on what was knowable at 04:10Z the mis-diagnosis was reasonable, and the response (use only the
venue you actually trade on; do not import external price levels) was correct and conservative. The
refinement worth carrying forward: an unexplained ~4% gap between your execution venue's price and the
outside world is a data-integrity condition in its own right and should be flagged as unresolved rather
than concluded as a venue characteristic. The action taken was `no_trade` either way, so nothing was lost.

**5. Demo-host price artifact flagged.** The BTCUSDT 1h bar opening 09:00Z prints a high of 95,804.10
against an open of 83,753.50 and a close of 84,625.90 — a ~14% wick with no counterpart in the other four
contracts, which also contaminates the 24h high field. It looks like a thin simulated-book print. This is
not cosmetic: exchange-side stop and take-profit orders on this account are resting orders in the same
environment, so a structural stop placed near such a level could be triggered by an artifact and register
as a real loss in the ledger. → Proposal 3.

## What worked / what did not

**Worked**

- Refusing to tighten the BNBUSDT stop from 764.61 (structure) to 772.78 (convenient size) — the exact
  failure the strategy's stop rule exists to prevent, correctly identified and named in the decision file.
- Requiring the same standard of the short as of the long, rather than reaching for a trade because the
  rejection bar was clean.
- Evidence recorded with specific, checkable numbers and named endpoints, which is what made this review
  possible at all on day 1.
- Self-auditing the stall and the thesis error and escalating both, unprompted, instead of leaving a clean
  record that would have hidden a 7-hour gap.

**Did not work**

- Scheduled execution: one slot missed, one slot's decision submitted 7 hours late, review off-time.
- The decision file's thesis went into the permanent record containing a wrong causal claim. The correction
  is attached, but anyone reading `decisions/` alone gets the wrong story. Nothing in the layout points
  from a decision file to a later correction.

## Hypotheses

Day 1. No closed trades, one decision — nothing here is a finding, and none of these yet meets the bar for
a lesson (3 trades or 5 decisions).

- *(n=1 decision)* The two-independent-category rule may reject the majority of setups in a trending,
  extended market, because trend and price_action will routinely disagree when price is pressed against a
  range high. If most no-trades over the next week carry the reason
  `conflicting_evidence_no_two_categories_agree`, that is worth understanding — but note the rule cannot be
  loosened by this review, and today's instance was the rule working, not failing.
- *(n=1 day, 3 executions)* Scheduled runs appear unreliable on this host. Testable immediately: slot
  coverage over the next 7 days is a countable number.
- *(n=1 observation)* The demo host produces isolated extreme wick prints. If a second one appears on any
  contract this week, treating demo high/low extremes as unusable for stop placement moves from caution to
  a working assumption.
