## L-002 — A stop narrower than recent bar noise is a position size, not a stop

**Hypothesis.** Before accepting any reward/risk figure, check the stop distance against **N, the largest 5m bar
range of the preceding hour**. If the stop is inside N, the ratio is an artifact of the geometry and the setup is
to be declined regardless of how well it scores. The same applies to a stop that sits just above or below a level
the market keeps touching, however many multiples of N of room it appears to have.

**Measure N over the FULL preceding hour, not over a convenient recent window.** *(Added 2026-09-23.)* On
2026-09-23 at 21:00 BNBUSDT's N over the twelve most recent closed 5m bars was 0.222%, making the 783.46 stop
look like a comfortable 2.89x N; over the full preceding hour N was **0.800%**, because the 12:00Z bar that
*created the setup* was itself the largest of the last 26 — so the stop was **0.80x N, sub-noise, and was that
bar's exact high.** The setup that defines the trade is usually the largest bar in the window, so the shorter
measurement systematically flatters exactly the setups most likely to be taken.

**And the flattery grows with time, which is the trap.** *(Added 2026-09-23.)* The 21:00 note predicted in
writing that by 22:00 the 12:00Z bar would age out and N would collapse toward **0.222%**, mechanically making
the same 783.46 stop look acceptable on the same structure. Measured at 22:00: **0.224%.** Nothing had happened
except sixty minutes. A stop inside the range of the bar that created the setup stays a position size however
long you wait; see **L-006** and **L-007**.

**A declined setup is resolved only by a price the market actually made.** *(Added 2026-09-24.)* A target or a
stop touched by a price that L-001 excludes as an artifact **resolves nothing** and does not advance the re-test
clock in either direction. On 2026-09-24 the 02:45Z BNBUSDT print at 780.90 touched the declined 09-23 23:00
long's **target** and the declined 09-23 23:00 short's **stop** at the identical price: on raw exchange prints
the long won and the short lost at the same tick. **The symmetry is the proof** — a clock that such a print could
advance could be advanced by noise proving the opposite case at the same instant. Both constructions remain live
and unresolved. This clause makes the clock harder to advance, never easier.

**Evidence (25 decisions; decisive in eleven consecutive checks):** an XRPUSDT long printing **4.615 net on a 0.060%
stop** against a 0.883% bar range — one fifteenth of a bar; **BNBUSDT clearing the floor long *and* short

stop **0.27x N** away (09-23 16:00); ETHUSDT's 1.12x N stop resting 0.21% above a level touched three times in

## L-003 — Requote before acting on a ratio, and distrust one that improves by drifting toward the stop

**Hypothesis.** Take a second quote several minutes after the first and recompute the reward/risk before any entry.
A ratio that has *improved* because price moved toward a fixed stop is not an improving trade — the improvement is
the risk shrinking, and it usually comes with the structural premise breaking. The requote can also legitimately
*worsen* a trade, by moving price away from the stop so the risk grows; chasing it then means widening risk to
keep the same target, which is equally a refusal.

**The drift can *rescue* a construction that previously failed, not merely inflate one that already passed.**

exact moment the trade stopped existing.

## L-004 — Run the span screen before pricing any ratio, and price the nearest structural target

**Hypothesis.** Before computing any reward/risk, measure N and measure the **clean structural span** available:
the distance from the structurally honest stop to the **nearest** structural target, with no intervening level
that has already rejected. A valid entry needs risk of at least N and reward of at least 1.5x risk, so that span
must be at least **2.5N plus roughly 0.14% of round-trip fee and slippage drag**. If the structure does not offer
it, stop computing — no entry price rescues it, because as the entry moves the stop becomes noise-safe and the
ratio collapses, or the ratio passes and the stop goes sub-noise. The two conditions move in opposite directions.

**A target is not valid if it is reached only by jumping intervening structure**, and this is the failure L-002
cannot see: L-002 stops the *stop* being chosen to fit the number, and this stops the *target* being chosen to fit
it. A target beyond the range extreme, beyond the end of the data, beyond an externally unconfirmed print, or in a
structural vacuum is not a target.

**A near-miss measured against a rolled-forward window is not a near-miss.** *(Added 2026-09-24.)* Three of the
narrowest misses in the experiment occurred on one day — **0.020pp** (00:00), **0.003pp** (09:00) and **0.077pp**
(21:00) — and none of them is evidence that the floor is nearly satisfiable. The 09:00 miss became **0.614pp**,
two hundred times larger, when re-measured against the 8h15m window containing the box actually being traded; the
21:00 miss was on a construction independently dead on target validity; and the 00:00 miss had every 1.5-net
target sitting in the vacuum below the 09-19 daily low. **Always state the miss against the setup-containing
window, and state it first.**

**Evidence (25 decisions; disqualified the check's own best candidate in eleven consecutive checks, in both
directions):** 09-23 00:00 derived the rule against a structural span of 1.185% where ~2.2% was required. Then
the escalation of what "passing" cost: **02:00** — the only passing construction targeted a level beyond **four**
1h highs that had each rejected within five hours; **09:00** — the only path to 1.5 targeted a price above the
highest print in the window; **16:00** — every passing construction depended on a print the outside world did not
confirm; **20:00** — the only passing target sat **below the 24h low** in a vacuum; **21:00** — ETHUSDT first
cleared 1.5 at "a price with no structure of any kind"; **22:00** — ETHUSDT refused on geometry alone with the
best book of the five. Then on 09-24: **09:00 — four constructions out of four, in both directions on both
readable contracts, all failing at their nearest structural target and all passing only by jumping structure that

## L-005 — Sample top-of-book notional at least three times, and refuse if the side the trade must cross is unquoted

## L-006 — Before treating a threshold crossing as a market event, check whether the measurement window moved

**Hypothesis.** Any number computed over a rolling window — the noise floor N, a 24h relative-performance figure,
a 24h high or low, an artifact scan over the last 30 bars — changes for two quite different reasons: the market
moved, or a bar entered or left the window. **On this host the second reason is common, and it is the more
persuasive of the two, because it tends to deliver the crossing exactly when a check has been waiting for one.**
Before acting on any threshold crossing, re-measure against a window that **contains the bar or event that
defines the setup**, and state explicitly what entered or left the window since the previous check. If the
crossing survives that re-measurement it is a market event; if it does not, it is arithmetic, and the trade is
refused.

This lesson generalises L-002's amendment from stop distance to every window-derived figure, and it is the reason
**L-001**'s exclusions do not lapse merely because a scan window rolls forward over a quiet stretch. **L-007**
extends it forwards in time.

**Evidence (25 decisions; 6 instances across 4 checks):**
- **BNBUSDT's noise floor, predicted and confirmed** (09-23 21:00 → 22:00). Forecast 0.222%, measured **0.224%**.
  The identical stop on identical structure read 1.76x N against the rolled-forward window and **0.49x N** against
  the window containing the bar whose high the stop *is*. The flattered version scored best on every number and
  was refused.

## L-007 — Name in advance which windows will roll before the next check, and which number that will flatter

**Hypothesis.** Window-induced flattery on this host is not merely common — it is **forecastable**, and
forecasting it in writing is what stops it working. Every check must state, before the next check runs: which bar
is about to leave which window, which measured figure will move as a result (N, the required span, a 24h extreme,
an artifact scan), and **in which direction**. The successor check must then measure that figure against the
window containing the bar that defines the setup **before** reading the rolled-forward value, and must say
whether the forecast was right.

The mechanism is one-sided, which is why it matters: a window rolling forward almost always *loosens* a measured
constraint. N shrinks when the big bar leaves. A 24h extreme softens when the spike leaves. An exclusion looks
stale when the prints leave. Nothing about the passage of time makes a setup harder, so an unforecast rolling
window delivers exactly one kind of surprise.

**A forecast may be conditional, and the strongest one so far was.** *(Added with the lesson, 2026-09-24.)* A
branch — "N collapses further **unless** X, in which case X binds" — is a stronger forecast than a point value,
because it tells the successor which of two quite different measurements to make before it looks at either.

**Evidence (25 decisions; 4 instances, 3 of them forecast in writing):**
- **0.222% forecast, 0.224% measured** — the 09-23 21:00 note told its successor that BNB's twelve-bar N would
  collapse and that an unchanged 783.46 stop on unchanged structure would look acceptable. It did, it was, and the
  22:00 check refused it.
- **"toward ~0.20%" forecast, 0.205% measured** — the 09-24 20:00 note made the same call on ETH one day later,
  naming the 09:30Z bar and both the 1h and 2h windows it would leave. The 21:00 check confirmed it and held the
  binding N at 0.551%.
- **The conditional branch, 09-24 21:00 → 22:00 — the strongest of the four.** The 21:00 note wrote that N
  would keep collapsing **unless a new impulse bar printed on the breakout, in which case that bar would become
  the setup-defining bar and the binding N with it**, and told its successor not to let a quiet stretch
  manufacture a pass. **Both halves fired.** The 0.549% 09:30Z bar left every window shorter than ~5h, *and* a
  new impulse bar printed at 13:35Z (0.468%, 3.18x the 5m median). N therefore did **not** fall to the ~0.19% a
  quiet stretch would have delivered: N(hour) = N(2h) = N(3h) = **0.468%**, N(5h) = 0.549%. The required span
  held at 1.310% long and 1.513% short.
- **Not forecast, and the largest of the four** — 09-24 09:00, where six silent overnight hours took BNB's N from
  0.403% to 0.159% and halved the required span. There was no warning to measure against; the check reconstructed
  the honest window by hand, which is exactly the work the forecast is meant to pre-empt.

**Why it can only make the AI more selective:** it adds a pre-commitment a candidate must survive. It cannot make
passable anything that was not already passable, and a figure that moves as forecast is a reason to refuse, never
a permission. → **P-010** asks Edward to make the transition visible automatically; until then this is discipline,
and it currently depends on one check having the foresight to warn the next one in prose.

**Day 5 record, stated honestly (2026-09-25).** Seven forecasts were checked today. **Four were confirmed**:

**All three misses were in the tightening direction.** Nothing loosened more than forecast, which fits the
